Enes Ayaz

Ph.D. candidate in power electronics, KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm

I am a doctoral student in the Division of Electric Power and Energy Systems, supervised by Staffan Norrga and Hans-Peter Nee. My research is about optimizing electric traction drives as a whole, mainly for heavy-duty vehicles. A drive is usually designed part by part, but a choice in one part changes the others: the dc-link voltage affects the battery, the charging and the cables, and the switching frequency moves losses between the inverter and the motor.

So I look at the battery, inverter, modulation and electric machine together. I work on inverter topologies and semiconductor choices, loss models and virtual prototyping, modulation methods that reduce common-mode voltage and dc-link capacitor stress, the harmonic losses that the inverter causes in the machine, and the health of power devices over their lifetime. The work is also experimental, with double-pulse measurements, a 250 kW, 1.2 kV prototype and a power-hardware-in-the-loop test bed.

I have worked in the HEMIZERO and PowerizeD projects, and I now work in Moore4Power.

Before KTH, I studied Electrical and Electronics Engineering at Middle East Technical University (B.Sc. 2019, M.Sc. 2022). At METU PowerLab, with Ozan Keysan, I worked on wireless power transfer, contactless field excitation of electrical machines and GaN-based integrated motor drives. The main idea of that work was to let one converter do two jobs, such as driving a motor and feeding a wireless link at the same time.
