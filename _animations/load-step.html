A synchronous motor on a stiff grid carries more load by increasing its power angle δ. When the load jumps, the rotor slows down for a moment, overshoots the new angle and swings back. Move the load slider to step the load.

Things to try

  • Step the load from 0.30 to 0.65 pu. δ swings past its new value and settles. The two shaded areas on the power curve are equal at the turning point.
  • Set the damping to zero and step again. The swing never dies out.
  • Step to more than the value under the curve, the largest stable step from rest. The accelerating area is too large, δ passes the unstable point and the machine loses synchronism.
  • Change the inertia. A heavier rotor swings more slowly, but how far it swings does not change.

Model

The classical model of a synchronous machine on an infinite bus, with normalized inertia \(M\), damping \(D\) and \(P_{max} = 3VE/X_s = 1\) pu:

\[ M\,\ddot\delta + D\,\dot\delta = P_{load} - P_{max}\sin\delta, \qquad \delta = \angle V - \angle E \]

A positive \(\dot\delta\) means the rotor is slower than synchronous speed. Without damping, a step from rest at \(\delta_0\) to the load \(P\) stays in synchronism when the equal-area criterion holds:

\[ \int_{\delta_0}^{\pi - \arcsin P} \left(P - \sin\delta\right) d\delta \le 0 \]

Electrical transients, saturation and stator resistance are neglected, and the cylindrical-rotor power curve is used. Time is normalized; one second on screen is two time units.
