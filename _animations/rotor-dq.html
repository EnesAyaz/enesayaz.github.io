Seen from the rotor, the stator currents of a synchronous machine are two constant numbers, \(i_d\) and \(i_q\). The angle of the current vector to the rotor sets the torque. Drag the blue current vector in the rotor frame on the right.

Things to try

  • Set the saliency to zero. Torque comes only from \(i_q\), and the best angle for a given current is 90°.
  • Make \(L_d\) smaller than \(L_q\), as in an interior permanent-magnet machine. The best angle moves past 90°: some negative \(i_d\) gives extra reluctance torque.
  • Make \(L_d\) larger than \(L_q\), as in a salient-pole wound-field machine. Now the best angle is below 90°.
  • Watch the stator while the rotor turns. The phase currents are sinusoidal, but in the rotor frame nothing moves.

Model

Normalized machine with \(\psi_f = 1\), one pole pair and \(L_q = 1\) pu. With the current of size \(I\) at angle \(\beta\) from the d axis,

\[ i_d = I\cos\beta, \quad i_q = I\sin\beta, \qquad T_e = \tfrac32\, p\,\left[\psi_f\, i_q + (L_d - L_q)\, i_d\, i_q\right] \]

The stator currents follow from the amplitude-invariant Park transform, \(i_k = I\cos(\theta_r + \beta - \alpha_k)\). MTPA marks the angle with the most torque for the present current. The rotor turns once in 7 seconds on screen.
