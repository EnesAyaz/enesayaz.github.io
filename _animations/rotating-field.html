--- title: Rotating stator field description: Three phase currents make one field that turns. Change the currents and watch the circle become an ellipse. topics: [Electrical machines] animation: rotating-field view: thumb order: 4 ---

Each phase winding makes a field that only pulses along its own axis. Fed with balanced three-phase currents, the three pulsating fields add up to one field of constant size that rotates. Lower the current of one phase and see what is left of the rotation.

Things to try

  • Keep the currents balanced. The blue vector has a constant length of 1.5 times the field of one phase, and its tip draws a circle.
  • Set phase c to zero. The tip now draws an ellipse: a forward field and a backward field rotate against each other.
  • Set two phases to zero. Only a pulsating field is left. It is made of equal forward and backward parts, 0.5 each.
  • Switch the sequence to acb. The field turns the other way, as a motor does when two supply wires are swapped.

Model

An ideal two-pole stator with sinusoidally distributed windings. The axes of phases a, b and c are at 0°, 120° and 240°. With phase currents \(i_k = I_k \cos(\theta - \varphi_k)\), the stator field, in units of the peak field of one phase, is

\[ \vec F = \sum_k i_k\, e^{j\alpha_k} = P\,e^{j\theta} + N\,e^{-j\theta}, \qquad P = \tfrac12 \sum_k I_k e^{j(\alpha_k - \varphi_k)}, \quad N = \tfrac12 \sum_k I_k e^{j(\alpha_k + \varphi_k)} \]

For balanced currents \(|P| = 1.5\) and \(|N| = 0\). The grey arrows show the fundamental of the radial air-gap field. A dot is current out of the screen. One electrical period takes 6 seconds on screen.
